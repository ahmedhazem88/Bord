## 1. Executive Summary

bord is a multi-tenant SaaS platform that digitizes corporate governance for Egyptian joint stock companies (S.A.E.), FRA-regulated non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs), CBE-regulated banks, and EGX-listed companies. It models a company's actual governance structure, chairman, MD, executive and non-executive directors, company secretary, officers, committees, and shareholders/GA members, including the fact that a single person routinely holds several governance capacities across several companies at once. On top of that structure, bord runs a rules engine encoding Egyptian Companies Law 159/1981, Capital Market Law 95/1992 and EGX listing rules, FRA Law 5/2022 and FRA Decree 100/2020 (the NBFI corporate governance code), CBE Law 194/2020 and the CBE bank-governance circular, and the EIoD Corporate Governance Code, so that scheduling, quorum, voting, disclosure, and record-keeping are checked against the law automatically rather than left to a secretary's memory or a law firm's periodic review.

Two loops that usually sit entirely off-platform are closed directly into that same compliance data. When the compliance engine flags a board-composition gap (e.g., a missing independent director under FRA Decree 100), a minimal professional-network layer lets the company post the opening and a vetted candidate apply, with an accepted candidate converting directly into a governance capacity, no re-entry. And when a remuneration committee sets director/committee compensation, bord calculates it from real attendance records, routes it through the same conflict-of-interest-aware approval workflow used for every other vote, and, for MVP, exports a payout-ready file rather than moving money itself, with the data model deliberately built so a payment-execution layer can be added in Phase 2 without a rewrite.

Onboarding is treated as two decoupled flows, not one combined form (Section 9, Journeys A and I): a company gets registered with its full foundational document set and its governance *history*, not just its current state, so the precedent database has real content from day one instead of starting empty; a person gets a portable identity, KYC-verified as who they are, with their actual board/committee positions carried by real `Capacity` records wherever the company is a bord tenant and self-declared everywhere else, decoupled from any one company, consistent with the platform's core person/capacity split.

Research (this pass, September 2026) confirms no dedicated Egyptian or Arab-world-built competitor exists in this exact space. Global board-portal vendors (Diligent, Convene, BoardPAC, Nasdaq Boardvantage, iBabs, Admincontrol) sell generic scheduling/e-voting/document workflows with no Egyptian legal logic; Egypt's one homegrown asset, E-Magles, is confirmed this pass to be an EGX-affiliated e-voting utility (built by EGID, EGX's technology subsidiary, a correction from the prior pass, which had left its ownership unconfirmed), not a governance platform; and no competitor found combines governance compliance with a structured board-seat marketplace. The wedge is legal depth plus workflow closure, not portal features.

The regulatory environment contains a near-term, architecture-defining constraint: Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020's cross-border-transfer licensing requirement pushes bord toward Egypt/MENA-region data hosting from day one (Section 5, Section 9), since the platform stores board members' PII and meeting recordings. A second, newly sharpened finding this pass: the commonly repeated claim that 100% shareholder attendance shortens GA notice from 21 to 5 days could not be confirmed as either codified statute or documented administrative practice, a materially more skeptical finding than the prior draft's "administrative practice, not statute," and one that changes how this claim must be presented in the product (Section 5).

## 2. Problem & Opportunity

Egyptian boards and committees currently run governance logistics, scheduling, agenda-setting, document circulation, minute-taking, quorum tracking, and regulatory-deadline tracking, through email, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, and physical board packs, with legal compliance checked manually by a company secretary or outside counsel (Section 4.3; this specific workaround claim is a reasoned inference from indirect evidence, not a directly sourced survey, flagged in Section 16). Three adjacent problems compound it: board and committee seats are filled almost entirely through personal networks, with no structured way for a company to signal a specific compliance-driven need or for a qualified candidate to be discovered outside their existing circle; director/committee remuneration is set and paid through ad hoc processes disconnected from the attendance and approval records that already exist once a company runs its meetings on a real governance platform; and a new company or a new director joining the platform has no structured way to bring their governance *history*, either the company's own past minutes and resolutions, or a person's own record of who they are and what positions they actually hold, into a system that would otherwise start from zero.

Two forces make the core opportunity a live one rather than a stale one:

- **Regulatory tightening is active, not historical.** FRA Decree 100/2020 imposed mandatory board-independence ratios and committee structures on NBFIs; CBE's amended bank-governance circular imposes comparable structural requirements on banks with CBE Governor approval required for board and senior-management appointments (Art. 120, Law 194/2020, reconfirmed this pass); GAFI rolled out electronic signature/payment functionality for corporate filings in 2025; PDPL 151/2020's executive regulations and enforcement grace period run out at the end of October 2026. Companies across three separate regulatory tracks (FRA, CBE, EGX/Companies Law) are being pushed toward digital, auditable governance processes at the same moment the tooling to do that natively for Egyptian law doesn't exist yet.
- **A verifiable gap in the vendor landscape.** Diligent has entered MENA with an Arabic UI and cites internally that only 55.6% of regional companies reported governance improvements over three years, evidence of unmet need, not of the gap being closed (Zawya, Diligent MENA launch). No vendor found offers Egyptian-law-aware compliance logic; all treat "compliance" as security certification (ISO 27001/SOC 2), not legal procedure-checking.

**Market sizing (bottom-up, assumptions stated explicitly, see Section 6 for full derivation):** roughly 1,800–2,300 companies in Egypt are a realistic serviceable market (EGX-listed + FRA-regulated NBFIs + CBE-regulated banks + an estimated multiple of larger private JSCs with formal boards), against a nominal ceiling of ~61,000 GAFI-identified listing-eligible entities, the large majority of which are too small to ever need this category of software. No credible Egypt-specific or MENA-specific board-governance-software market-size figure exists in public sources; global board-portal market data ($3.68B in 2026, 11.8% CAGR, Research and Markets) is cited only as a directional proxy, not as an Egypt estimate.

## 3. PESTEL Analysis

Only factors materially relevant to this product and geography are included.

**Political.** Four regulators have direct authority over the product's subject matter: GAFI (commercial register, AoA filings, company formation), FRA (NBFI licensing and the Decree 100 governance code), EGX/the Capital Market Authority function within FRA (listing and disclosure rules), and CBE (bank licensing, board-appointment approval, and bank-governance circulars). GAFI and EGX have an active, recent cooperation push (identifying ~61,000 listing-eligible companies, June 2026), a signal of policy direction toward formalizing more companies' governance, favorable for bord's addressable market. GAFI/FRA/EGX/CBE could become channel partners or a source of an official "compliant platform" endorsement later, worth pursuing but not assumed in the MVP plan.

**Economic.** Egypt's macro environment (currency volatility, inflation) affects two things directly: willingness to pay in USD-pegged SaaS terms (argues for EGP-denominated, tiered pricing rather than US-benchmark pricing, see Section 6), and the cost of maintaining Egypt-region cloud infrastructure, which may carry a premium over US/EU hyperscaler regions. Currency risk should be hedged by invoicing in EGP with periodic re-pricing rather than long-term fixed USD contracts. The board-seat hiring module is modeled as upside revenue, not baked into the base ARR projection (Section 6).

**Social.** Governance formalization is a trust-building signal for Egyptian companies seeking foreign investment or EGX listing, so adoption is partly reputational, not just operational efficiency. Senior board members (chairmen, NEDs) skew older and less software-native than the operational staff of most SaaS buyers, a UX constraint (Persona 3), not just a market observation. Board and committee seats being filled almost entirely through personal networks today is itself a social fact worth naming: a structured, compliance-aware hiring layer is a genuine behavior change, not just a feature request, adoption of that module should be expected to lag the compliance core's adoption. Individual onboarding's self-declaration step (Section 9, Journey I) runs into the same social reality from the other direction: asking a senior director to declare every position they hold, in a system they didn't build trust in yet, is itself an adoption hurdle, not a form-filling formality.

**Technological.** Two enabling shifts make this newly viable: (1) LLMs are now capable of grounded, citation-backed legal guidance when scoped to a curated corpus, which is what makes the AI governance guidance, the discussion-guide generation, the pre-vote conflict check, and the document-extraction step in both onboarding journeys credible rather than a liability risk if built correctly (retrieval-grounded, human-confirmed, never freeform); (2) GAFI's 2025 electronic-signature rollout removes a prior hard blocker (wet-signature-only filings) for at least some filing types. Platform dependency risk exists on the video-conferencing SDK vendor, the LLM API vendor, and the KYC-verification vendor (new this pass, since individual onboarding now depends on one); all are mitigated architecturally (ARCHITECTURE.md).

**Environmental.** Not materially applicable, pure software product, no meaningful environmental footprint or exposure beyond standard data-center energy use.

**Legal.** Covered in depth in Section 5; this entry flags only what isn't already a dedicated section: Egypt's E-Signature Law No. 15 of 2004 governs whether e-signed resolutions/minutes are legally equivalent to wet-signature originals, reconfirmed this pass (Section 5) but still needs local-counsel verification of full scope before marketing e-signature/e-voting outputs as binding without a fallback wet-signature path (Section 16).

### Notice & quorum exceptions catalog (re-verified this pass)

The exact question asked was whether the 21-day OGM notice period drops to 5 days at 100% attendance. That specific figure does not hold up under fresh scrutiny, see the row above, now downgraded further than the prior pass's already-skeptical finding. What does hold up, all quoted from researched primary or primary-quoting text rather than inferred, is a real set of exceptions and carve-outs, catalogued here rather than left scattered across the table:

- **OGM second-call quorum waiver** (Exec. Regs Art. 213): if the first-call quorum (25–50% of capital) isn't met, a second meeting within the statutory window is valid regardless of how much capital is represented, a full quorum waiver, not a reduced threshold.
- **EGM two-tier quorum** (Companies Law 159/1981): first call needs ≥50% of capital; second call drops to ≥25%, within 30 days of the first.
- **Board minority-call mechanism** (Art. 80): one-third of directors may request the chairman call a meeting, reconfirmed High confidence this pass. What is *not* confirmed is a self-convening right if the chairman doesn't respond; treat that half of the mechanism as a configurable product default, not a quoted legal right (see the table row above).
- **Shareholder-requisitioned EGM** (Art. 70(1)): ≥10% capital holders can compel the board to convene within one month, with a GAFI-petition fallback if it doesn't, reconfirmed High confidence this pass.
- **What was searched for and still not found:** a codified notice-period reduction tied to full attendance or unanimous shareholder consent (now more firmly unconfirmed than before, see above), a distinct committee-level requisition threshold, a fixed day-count for board pre-meeting notice ("adequate" information is the only standard found), and, new this pass, the specific statutory basis for the Art. 80 self-convening right. These stay open items (Section 16) rather than encoded rules.

### Governance-record retention

No Egyptian statute was found, in either research pass, that states a retention period specifically for board minutes, committee resolutions, GA minutes, or voting records. The only clearly stated, sourced figure anywhere in the surrounding legal framework is the Commercial Code's general bookkeeping rule (Law 17/1999, Art. 26): commercial ledgers and related correspondence must be kept **5 years** from ledger closure, a real, high-confidence figure, but a general bookkeeping rule applied to governance records only by analogy, not a governance-specific mandate. **Interim product decision, pending counsel confirmation:** bord retains governance records (votes, minutes, resolutions, attendance) indefinitely, or at minimum 10 years past a company's subscription cancellation, reflecting standard corporate/litigation-evidence practice rather than the thinner 5-year bookkeeping analogy, see Section 13's retention-vs-erasure edge case. PDPL 151/2020's storage-limitation and erasure principles are not in hard conflict with this: a genuine statutory or evidentiary retention duty is a recognized legitimate basis to retain, so PDPL requires minimizing/de-identifying the personal data embedded in a record (attendee names, voting details) where legally possible, not deleting the governance record itself.

### Person-searchable affiliation registry, closed question, reconfirmed this pass

Researched specifically (both passes) whether GAFI, FRA, or EGX maintain any registry searchable **by person** (not just by company) to verify a candidate's board/committee affiliations across companies. **Confirmed again this pass: no such registry exists or is accessible to a private company.** GAFI's commercial-registry and EGX/FRA systems are consistently described as company-centric, director names surface only once a specific company record is opened, not as a cross-company index. Egypt's 2020 Beneficial Owners Register is similarly company-maintained and restricted mainly to judicial officers on request, not publicly person-searchable (new detail confirmed this pass). This closes the door on any automated cross-registry verification approach; conflict/verification checking stays scoped to bord's own tenant `Capacity` data (FR-32), with self-declaration as the honest fallback for everything outside it, a boundary the individual-onboarding journey (Journey I) exists specifically to make workable given this constraint, not a workaround for a feature that simply wasn't built yet.

**Concrete blockers flagged for the roadmap:**

1. **PDPL cross-border data transfer licensing** (enforcement grace period ends ~end of October 2026) is the single largest architecture-defining constraint: bord must default to Egypt/MENA-region data hosting for board member PII and meeting recordings, or obtain a PDPC cross-border transfer license before hosting elsewhere.
2. **GAFI's role in ratifying general assembly minutes** is unverified as to whether it requires direct system integration. MVP scope treats GAFI filing as export-ready document generation, not direct system integration.
3. **Several Companies Law board-meeting mechanics** (exact board quorum percentage, exact GA notice-period day count, the Art. 80 self-convening detail, the board/committee meeting-requisition threshold) were not confirmed against primary statutory text in either research pass. All are architected as **configurable per company's rules profile**, not hardcoded constants.
4. **NBFI subtype jurisdiction boundaries** (FRA vs. CBE) should be reconfirmed per subtype, particularly for microfinance, which has had jurisdictional shifts historically.
5. **CBE's primary regulations library was not directly accessible during either research pass.** Every CBE-specific rule above is Medium or Low confidence and needs primary-source retrieval (the actual 2021-amended circular text, and the Article 56 large-exposure regulation) before any CBE rule is presented to a bank customer as authoritative rather than a starting default.
6. **KYC-vendor selection for individual onboarding (Journey I) is a new dependency this pass**, not yet evaluated for Egypt-specific national-ID verification coverage; needed before Phase 4 (Architecture & Tech Stack) finalizes.

### Independent director vs. shareholder-representative director, criteria and the gap in Egyptian law (new this pass)

Researched specifically this pass, with source URLs, to support FR-57's compliance check. The finding splits cleanly into what Egyptian regulation actually defines and what it doesn't.

| Regime | What it establishes | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Egyptian Corporate Governance Code (EFSA Board Resolution 84/2016) | Affirmative independent-director definition: non-executive; not a shareholder; no material business relationship with the company; no blood/marriage relation within the second degree to shareholders, directors, or executives; not a senior officer, advisor, or external auditor of the company within the prior 3 years; maximum 6 consecutive years' tenure; compensated only as a board member; explicitly **not** "a representative of the company's owners" | High, primary-quoting source, direct quote available |
| FRA Decree 177/2023 (EGX listing/delisting rules) | Independent director additionally disqualified above a **1% shareholding/voting-rights threshold**, for EGX-listed companies specifically | Medium, secondary law-firm summary (Mondaq); specific article numbers not independently verified |
| FRA Decree 200/2025 (insurance/reinsurance NBFIs) | Same 1% disqualifying threshold; board minimum 5 members, majority non-executive, minimum 2 independent, minimum 25% female, Chairman/CEO split required | Medium, secondary summary (ADSERO); specific article numbers not independently verified |
| CBE bank-governance circular | No CBE text found distinguishing "independent" from "shareholder-representative" specifically; only the general non-executive/independent-count requirements already in the CBE table above | Low, this distinction appears simply unaddressed in what's publicly indexed for banks |
| Companies Law 159/1981 and its amendments | No affirmative statutory "shareholder-representative director" category with its own duties or nominee-voting rules; cumulative voting (already catalogued above) is a voting *mechanism*, not a reserved board seat | Confirmed absent |

**The core finding a compliance check has to be honest about:** "independent director" is a real, citable legal category in Egyptian regulation. "Shareholder representative" is not. It exists only negatively, as the category an independent director must not fall into. This is why `Capacity.directorCategory` (ARCHITECTURE.md §4) is built as a platform-defined classification rather than presented as a second regulated status alongside independence, and why FR-57's compliance check is advisory-only rather than a hard block: the platform can verify a director against the independence criteria with real citations, but it cannot cite a statute for what "shareholder representative" is supposed to mean, so a false-positive miscategorization flag is a real risk the UI must not overstate as a confirmed violation.

### Future-state: regulator-database and labor/social-insurance cross-checking, forward-looking, not currently buildable (new this pass)

Researched specifically to inform FR-56's forward architecture, not because this pass found anything newly buildable. Two distinct questions, both confirmed closed for now:

**Company-affiliation cross-check.** No API or bulk-access channel exists for GAFI's commercial registry, FRA's NBFI filings, or EGX/EGID's disclosure filings that would let a private-sector platform query "what companies is this person a director of." Third-party aggregators (OpenCorporates' GAFI mirror, InfobelPRO, businessdataguide) scrape or license-aggregate registry data rather than use a sanctioned API, which is itself evidence no such channel exists. Egypt's "Digital Egypt" program has built real institutional data-exchange infrastructure elsewhere (Nafeza for customs, Meeza for payments, documented NOSI/Digital-Egypt-Portal automation), so a future GAFI data-sharing product is plausible, but no such program, pilot, or public roadmap targeting person-to-company mapping was found.

**Employment/social-insurance cross-check.** The National Organization for Social Insurance (NOSI) has digitized its filings, a technical precondition for future queryability, but no public or institutional employment-verification API was found. Egypt's credit bureau, I-Score (CBE-licensed), is the clearest existing model of a regulator-sanctioned, institution-restricted data-sharing utility in Egypt's financial sector, but its data sources are lending records, not employment or social-insurance records; nothing found indicates it does income or employment verification as a product. Commercial background-check practice in Egypt relies on manual/documentary verification and direct employer reference calls, not any government database query.

**What this means for the build:** `ExternalVerificationCheck` (ARCHITECTURE.md §4) is modeled now, referencing `SelfDeclaredPosition`, with every status permanently `not_available` or `pending_integration` in this build. No code path may claim a position was externally verified, and no UI surface may imply one exists (AGENTS.md guardrail). This is a provision for a future integration, not a partially-built one.

## 7. Execution Risk

- **Team/skill gap:** the compliance rules engine and AI governance-guidance feature both require close, ongoing collaboration with Egyptian corporate/capital-markets counsel, a non-engineering dependency the lean team must budget for as a real, recurring cost line. CBE as a second, heavier-weight regulatory track increases this dependency: the Medium/Low-confidence CBE rules need primary-source retrieval and sign-off before any bank customer goes live. This pass adds a second dependency of the same shape: the Art. 80 self-convening detail and the (now further downgraded) 100%-attendance claim both need direct counsel confirmation before either is ever presented as settled, not just re-searched again next quarter.
- **Technical risk:** the AI governance-guidance feature, the discussion-guide generator, the pre-vote conflict check, and the document-extraction step used in both onboarding journeys are all built on the same retrieval-grounded, human-confirmed pattern and share the same risk profile, a wrong extraction from an uploaded AoA, or wrong legal guidance on quorum, disclosure, or a precedent conflict, could cause real harm to a customer. Mitigated architecturally (retrieval-grounded only, always cites source or flags "unconfirmed," degrades gracefully to "no answer" rather than guessing, human confirmation required before any extracted fact is applied), but needs a dedicated pre-launch evaluation suite, not just a demo.
- **Go-to-market risk:** the first 100 users will not come from inbound SaaS marketing, Egyptian board decisions are relationship-driven. The realistic channel is direct relationships with company secretaries, corporate-secretarial/governance advisory firms, and EIoD as a credibility partner. The minimal network/hiring module is a second, genuinely different growth motion layered on top of that relationship-led enterprise sale, a marketplace needs both sides to reach critical mass, and shipping it minimally in MVP is a real bet that a thin version is still useful enough to seed that flywheel rather than sitting empty.
- **Timing risk:** favorable on the compliance core, PDPL enforcement tightening and FRA Decree 100/CBE's mandates create forcing functions pushing companies toward compliant digital processes now. Risk is on the downside if global vendors (Diligent, Convene) localize Egyptian legal content faster than bord can build distribution.
- **Dependency risk:** video-conferencing SDK vendor, LLM API vendor, PDPC licensing timeline, and, new this pass, a KYC-verification vendor for individual onboarding (Journey I) are the material external dependencies. CBE's own regulations library being inaccessible in both research passes to date is a concrete, recurring dependency risk in its own right: primary-source CBE rule text needs to be obtained (likely via counsel or a direct CBE inquiry) before the bank segment can be marketed as fully supported.

## 8. User Personas

### Persona 1, The Company Secretary (Nourhan, Governance Officer)
**Role/context:** Full-time company secretary or governance officer at a mid-size FRA-regulated leasing company; often also serves informally as secretary for one or two committees, and is the person who actually runs company onboarding (Journey A) when the company first adopts bord. **Primary goal:** run a compliant, defensible governance calendar without personally memorizing every quorum/notice/disclosure rule, and, at onboarding, bring the company's real governance history into the system rather than starting from a blank slate. **Current workaround:** a personal spreadsheet tracking meeting dates and filing deadlines, cross-checked against outside counsel memos, with board packs assembled and emailed manually (inference, Section 4, flagged as an open question). **Pain points:** manually recalculating quorum and notice-period deadlines for every meeting type; assembling and version-controlling board packs by email; producing an audit trail after the fact rather than during the meeting; today, a new tool means re-typing years of prior minutes and resolutions from memory or paper files if she wants any historical context at all. **Tech comfort:** high, this is the platform's power user and primary daily operator. **Constraints:** works in both Arabic and English documents; needs the platform's authoritative language for legal documents to be Arabic with an English working translation. **Churn driver:** if the compliance engine's rule values are wrong or unconfigurable for her specific company's AoA/bylaws, or if onboarding asks her to abandon years of governance history rather than bring it in, she'll go back to manual tracking rather than trust the platform.

### Persona 2, The Portfolio Non-Executive Director (Karim, multi-board NED)
**Role/context:** Sits as a non-executive/independent director on 3–4 boards simultaneously, plus 2 committee memberships across different companies, the persona whose individual-onboarding experience (Journey I) matters most, since he'll declare positions at companies that aren't bord tenants at all. **Primary goal:** never miss a meeting, vote, or disclosure obligation across his whole portfolio, and have his actual cross-company record recognized once rather than re-entered at every company that invites him. **Current workaround:** personal assistant manually cross-references multiple companies' emailed invitations against his calendar. **Pain points:** scheduling conflicts across companies are discovered late; no unified view of pending votes/actions across all his capacities; independence-status tracking is entirely manual; every new company he joins asks him to re-state his other board positions from scratch. **Tech comfort:** moderate, often delegates to an assistant. **Constraints:** senior, time-poor, expects mobile-first, low-friction interactions. **Churn driver:** if switching between his multiple company "capacities" inside the app is confusing or slow, or if onboarding feels like yet another background-check form rather than something that pays off in convenience, he'll revert to his assistant's manual process, this persona directly justifies both the multi-capacity identity model and the decoupled, once-only individual-onboarding flow.

### Persona 3, The Chairman/Board Leader (Dr. Hassan, Chairman)
**Role/context:** Chairman of an EGX-listed company's board, also chairs the Nomination & Remuneration committee. **Primary goal:** run efficient, well-documented meetings that withstand regulatory and shareholder scrutiny. **Current workaround:** relies entirely on the company secretary and legal counsel. **Pain points:** limited real-time visibility into whether quorum is genuinely met; wants a simple, high-level compliance "health" view rather than granular detail. **Tech comfort:** low-to-moderate. **Constraints:** needs large-text, minimal-navigation, high-trust UI. **Churn driver:** any interaction requiring more than 2–3 taps to accomplish a routine task will push him back to delegating entirely to his secretary, this persona argues against a feature-dense default view, including in the remuneration-approval, hiring, and onboarding flows.

### Persona 4, The Audit Committee Chair (Mona, Independent Director & CFO-background)
**Role/context:** Independent director serving as Audit Committee chair at an FRA-regulated insurance company, the committee-head perspective, distinct from Persona 11's committee-member perspective below. **Primary goal:** discharge fiduciary/compliance duties defensibly. **Current workaround:** committee-specific tracking largely absent. **Pain points:** no systematic way to track the 6-year auditor tenure cap or confirm committee independence composition stays intact. **Tech comfort:** high. **Constraints:** needs committee-specific views, filtered to only her committee's agenda items and alerts. **Churn driver:** if the platform can't distinguish committee-scoped obligations from full-board ones, it adds noise rather than removing it.

### Persona 5, The Compliance/Legal Officer (Youssef, in-house counsel)
**Role/context:** In-house legal/compliance officer at an EGX-listed company. **Primary goal:** catch governance violations before a regulator does. **Current workaround:** periodic manual compliance review cycles. **Pain points:** by the time a manual review catches an issue, it may already be a live violation. **Tech comfort:** high. **Constraints:** needs proactive alerting, not just on-demand reporting. **Churn driver:** false-positive-heavy alerts will get the feed muted within weeks, doubly true given the pre-vote conflict check (FR-44), which must be advisory and precise or Youssef will start overriding it reflexively.

### Persona 6, The Multi-Client Admin (Sherif, Corporate Secretarial Firm Partner)
**Role/context:** Partner at a corporate secretarial/governance advisory firm managing governance administration for 15–20 client companies under retainer, often the one actually driving Journey A (company onboarding) on a client's behalf, given the firm's existing custody of AoAs, bylaws, and historical minutes. **Primary goal:** run all client companies' governance calendars from one place with strict data segregation between clients. **Current workaround:** separate manual processes and files per client. **Pain points:** no visibility across his client portfolio without opening each client's separate files; onboarding 15–20 clients one at a time, each with its own document set and history, is a real operational cost he needs the platform to make lighter, not heavier. **Tech comfort:** high. **Constraints:** must never see one client's confidential board pack bleed into another client's view. **Churn driver:** any cross-client data leakage ends the relationship immediately, directly justifies strict row-level tenant isolation and is also bord's most promising B2B2B distribution channel.

### Persona 7, The Shareholder Relations Officer (Sara, GA logistics owner)
**Role/context:** Handles shareholder relations and general assembly logistics at a larger private JSC or smaller EGX-listed company, distinct from the board-facing Company Secretary role, though the two roles are sometimes combined at smaller companies. **Primary goal:** run a compliant GA cycle end to end, notice publication, quorum, shareholding-weighted voting, minutes, with zero tolerance for a miscalculated quorum or vote tally, since GA outcomes are externally visible to shareholders in a way board decisions aren't. **Current workaround:** a manual shareholder registry spreadsheet, reconciled by hand against registrar extracts, with weighted-vote tallying done manually during the meeting. **Pain points:** reconciling shareholding percentages from multiple sources; tracking the two-newspaper-publication notice requirement as an external, non-platform task; no systematic way to flag a shareholding held through an omnibus/custodian account that can't yet be individually verified. **Tech comfort:** high. **Constraints:** needs the system to clearly distinguish verified shareholdings from ones pending registrar confirmation, never silently treating them the same. **Churn driver:** a single publicly-visible tallying error is far more damaging to her professionally than an internal board-meeting glitch would be to Nourhan, she needs more conservative, more visible error-handling than the board-meeting flows, not the same defaults.

### Persona 8, The Director Candidate (Yara, aspiring independent director)
**Role/context:** A finance or industry professional building a portfolio non-executive career, currently holds one independent NED seat and is looking for a second, ideally an audit-committee-qualified seat. **Primary goal:** discover board/committee openings that genuinely match her expertise and independence eligibility, and get a fair, verifiable hearing rather than relying entirely on personal introductions. **Current workaround:** word-of-mouth only. **Pain points:** no structured way to prove her independence status and board history to a company she doesn't already know; no market signal on what boards are actually looking for. **Tech comfort:** high. **Constraints:** her public profile's independence claim carries real weight, so the profile must pull independence/other-board-count from her actual verified bord capacity records wherever she has any, and show anything else as self-declared from her own individual-onboarding declaration (Journey I), never blurred together. **Churn driver:** stale postings, no company follow-up after applying, or a platform that can't distinguish her verified history from an unverified new user's self-declared claims.

### Persona 9, The Head of Internal Audit (Layla, internal audit function head)
**Role/context:** Runs the internal audit function at an EGX-listed manufacturing company, reporting functionally to the Audit Committee (Persona 4's committee) and administratively to the CEO, a dual-reporting line that needs to be visible in the platform's structure builder so it isn't mistaken for a normal executive reporting line. **Primary goal:** get audit findings in front of the Audit Committee on schedule, with a clean record that the committee actually reviewed and acted on them. **Current workaround:** a separate audit-tracking spreadsheet, manually cross-referenced against board/committee meeting dates. **Pain points:** no structural link today between an audit finding and the committee agenda item that's supposed to close it out; committee reporting cadence tracked by memory rather than a compliance-calendar rule. **Tech comfort:** high. **Constraints:** needs a capacity type that's committee-facing but not itself a committee member or director, an attendee/reporter role, not a voting one. **Churn driver:** if the platform can't distinguish "reports to this committee" from "sits on this committee," she'll keep the audit-tracking spreadsheet running in parallel.

### Persona 10, The Money-Laundering Reporting Officer (Tarek, MLRO)
**Role/context:** MLRO at an FRA-regulated NBFI, a role FRA/AML regulation requires the company to designate by name, distinct from the general compliance officer role Persona 5 (Youssef) holds elsewhere. **Primary goal:** maintain a defensible, regulator-ready record that AML-relevant governance obligations are being met on schedule. **Current workaround:** AML-specific compliance tracking kept entirely separate from general board administration. **Pain points:** no way today to prove a specific board resolution approved the current AML policy version without manually pulling minutes. **Tech comfort:** moderate-to-high. **Constraints:** the MLRO designation needs to be a distinct, named capacity type so a company can prove to FRA that the legally-required role is actually filled and who holds it, at any point in time. **Churn driver:** if the MLRO capacity is indistinguishable from any other officer role, the one piece of evidence FRA actually asks for isn't reliably reconstructable.

### Persona 11, The Committee Member, non-chair (Hany, Risk Committee member)
**Role/context:** Non-executive director serving as an ordinary member (not chair) of the Risk Committee at a CBE-regulated bank, alongside a full board seat at a different company, the everyday committee-member experience, as distinct from Persona 4's committee-chair perspective. **Primary goal:** show up prepared to committee meetings without the chair's administrative overhead. **Current workaround:** relies entirely on the committee secretary/chair to push him what he needs. **Pain points:** a committee-scoped view that shows only his committee's agenda and materials, not the full board's, currently doesn't exist anywhere he uses. **Tech comfort:** moderate. **Constraints:** this is the persona that most directly stress-tests FR-23's committee-scoped RBAC. **Churn driver:** any interface that doesn't clearly separate "your committee" from "everything at this company" makes him fall back on being told what to do by the chair rather than using the platform proactively.

## 9. User Journeys

Each journey is walked step by step; the cross-check against functional requirements (Section 12) is called out explicitly at the end of each. Journey A (company onboarding) and Journey I (individual onboarding) are the two most substantially revised/expanded journeys this pass, reflecting the master prompt's dedicated Onboarding section.

### Journey A, Company onboarding, including governance history (Nourhan, Company Secretary)
1. **Trigger:** Nourhan's leasing company (FRA-regulated NBFI) decides to adopt bord. She, as the authorized member kicking off adoption, creates the company tenant.
2. She selects the regulatory profile (NBFI, leasing subtype; profiles also include CBE-regulated bank and the two-track "bank + EGX-listed" combination) and a subscription tier; the correct rule set loads immediately.
3. She uploads the company's **full foundational document set** to the Document Vault, not a partial one: commercial register extract, Articles of Association, Shareholders' Agreement where one exists, internal bylaws, tax registration certificate, and identity/appointment documentation for every currently sitting director and officer.
4. **Critically, at this same step, not a later, optional add-on, she also uploads the company's governance history**: previous board and GA minutes and resolutions, as far back as she has them. The system runs AI-assisted structural extraction across everything uploaded (board composition, notice-period language, the shareholding table, and dates/attendees/outcomes pulled from the historical minutes and resolutions), proposing extracted facts for her to confirm, never auto-applying them.
5. The confirmed historical minutes and resolutions are used specifically to **seed the precedent database (FR-43) with real content from day one**, rather than leaving a new company's precedent search empty for however long it takes to accumulate a history on-platform.
6. She builds the governance structure: adds the Chairman, MD, 4 NEDs (2 of whom are also committee members at other companies), the Audit and Risk committees mandated by Decree 100 (each instantiated from bord's required-committee taxonomy for her regulatory profile, Governance duties absorbed into the Audit committee per the reconfirmed FRA reading in Section 5, not always a separate committee, not built from scratch), and assigns capacities with start dates and independence flags, including the distinct MLRO capacity FRA regulation requires her company to name. Wherever a director or officer already has a bord identity from their own individual onboarding (Journey I), their capacity attaches to that existing person record rather than creating a duplicate.
7. The system runs an automatic composition check against the loaded rules (majority non-executive, ≥50% independent non-executives, chair/CEO split, MLRO designated) and flags any gap immediately.
8. **If her company falls outside mandatory jurisdiction** (a large private JSC with no FRA/CBE/EGX trigger), the same required-committee taxonomy and composition checks are offered as a recommended, skippable standard rather than a gate, she can choose to hold the company to the mandatory-tier bar anyway and generate a self-certified compliance score/report reflecting that (FR-48).
9. **Later, if the board wants a discretionary committee** (Investment, Steering, or another non-required type), standing it up isn't a separate admin action: it's proposed and voted on as a normal board resolution, and the committee is only instantiated once that resolution passes.
10. **Friction point:** if a person invited doesn't yet have a bord account, an invite email/SMS is sent with a single-step claim flow that routes them into Journey I (individual onboarding) if they're new to the platform entirely, or straight into accepting the new capacity if they already have a bord identity.
11. **Ends:** company tenant is live, governance structure is modeled, the precedent database has real historical content, and Nourhan sees a baseline compliance status.
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-1 (tenant/company onboarding), FR-2 (document vault + extraction), FR-2a (historical-minutes upload and precedent seeding, new this pass), FR-3 (governance structure builder), FR-4 (composition check), FR-25 (subscription billing), FR-28 (CBE regulatory profile support), FR-47 (committee taxonomy + resolution-gated creation, steps 6/9), FR-48 (voluntary compliance score, step 8), FR-57 (director-category classification, part of the governance structure builder's capacity detail), FR-60 (committee decision-quorum config, part of the same builder's committee detail).

### Journey B, Schedule and convene a board meeting (Nourhan + Karim, portfolio NED)
1. **Trigger:** quarterly board-meeting cadence requirement is approaching, **or** a configurable number/percentage of directors invokes the member-requisitioned-meeting threshold set in the company's rules profile (FR-42), configured, per Section 5's downgraded Art. 80 finding, as a company-set default rather than an asserted statutory self-convening right.
2. Nourhan opens the Meeting Scheduler; the system pulls each invitee's synced calendar free/busy (Google Calendar or Microsoft 365, where connected), combines it with their standing timing preferences (preferred days/times, blackout dates), and cross-references other company commitments where they hold multiple bord capacities, then proposes 2–3 concrete slot options instead of asking her to pick a date cold.
3. She selects one of the proposed slots (or a different date); the system validates it against the applicable notice-period rule (the Medium-confidence 21-day default from Section 5) and flags if the proposed date doesn't leave enough notice.
4. She builds the agenda: the system suggests recurring compliance-calendar items plus carried-over items. For each new item, she picks from a taxonomy of common agenda topics (financial reporting, related-party transactions, committee-report reception, policy approval, etc.), the taxonomy surfaces the topics her own company raises most often first, which drops in editable preset template language, or she starts from a blank item; either way she tags it with a disclosure-sensitivity level and a topic category, which pre-selects the applicable voting threshold for that specific item.
5. **Before the agenda is finalized**, each substantive item is checked against the precedent database (now populated with real history from Journey A, not empty) and the applicable law/AoA/bylaw text: the system surfaces related prior resolutions and any potential conflict as an advisory flag Nourhan must acknowledge before publishing, never a block, and generates a discussion guide per item.
6. Invitations go out (email/SMS/push, bilingual); Karim receives his on mobile, sees it already resolved against his other three companies' calendars, and accepts.
7. **Friction point:** if Karim doesn't respond within a configurable window, the system escalates to Nourhan.
8. **Ends:** meeting is scheduled, agenda and discussion guides are locked a set number of days before the meeting, and all invitees have visibility into both.
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-5, FR-6, FR-7, FR-8, FR-9, FR-26, FR-40, FR-41, FR-42, FR-43, FR-44, FR-45, FR-46.

**Cross-cutting requirements not tied to a single journey step:** FR-23 (capacity-scoped RBAC) and FR-24 (strict tenant data isolation) are enforced throughout every journey. FR-27 (board evaluation surveys) intentionally has no MVP journey driving it, consistent with its Could priority. FR-31 (GA data model extensibility for future public-AGM scale) is a data-model property Journey F's schema must satisfy, verified by architecture review rather than a journey walkthrough.

### Journey C, Conduct the meeting: embedded conferencing, live quorum, and voting
1. **Trigger:** scheduled meeting time arrives.
2. Attendees join via the embedded video module; the system tracks live attendance and computes real-time quorum status against the applicable quorum rule, visible to the Chairman and Secretary throughout.
3. Dr. Hassan (Chairman) walks the agenda using the discussion guide generated in Journey B for each item; for each voting item, the e-voting panel opens using the topic-appropriate voting threshold. If an item involves a director's own compensation or a related-party matter, the system automatically flags that director for recusal per Art. 74 and excludes their vote.
4. Votes are cast (board meetings: one member, one vote; GA-type meetings: weighted by shareholding) and tallied in real time; the resolution outcome and vote record are captured immutably.
5. **Friction point, third-party outage:** if the video SDK fails mid-meeting, the system falls back to a manual roll-call quorum/attendance capture mode built into the meeting room UI.
6. AI-assisted draft minutes are generated in real time, for the Secretary's review, never auto-finalized without human approval.
7. **Ends:** meeting concludes with a locked vote record, a draft-minutes document pending secretary approval, and an updated compliance status; the meeting and its resolutions are indexed into the precedent database for future retrieval.
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-10, FR-11, FR-12, FR-13, FR-14, FR-40, FR-41, FR-43.

### Journey D, Post-meeting compliance and disclosure
1. **Trigger:** meeting concludes; draft minutes and vote record exist.
2. Nourhan reviews and approves the final minutes; the system e-signs/timestamps them and archives them immutably, hash-chained into the audit log.
3. The Compliance Center automatically checks the meeting's outcomes against disclosure-timeline rules and creates tracked filing tasks with deadlines, assigned to Youssef.
4. Youssef reviews the auto-generated, filing-ready export and completes the actual filing through the relevant external channel.
5. **Friction point:** if a deadline is missed despite the alert, the system escalates to a configurable secondary approver.
6. **Ends:** filing task is marked complete, the compliance record shows a fully documented, timestamped trail from meeting to filing.
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-15, FR-16, FR-17, FR-18.

### Journey E, Ongoing AI governance guidance and violation monitoring
1. **Trigger:** ongoing, the Compliance Center continuously evaluates the company's live governance state against its rules profile.
2. Youssef asks the AI governance assistant a natural-language question; the assistant answers only from the curated, versioned Egyptian-law corpus, cites the specific source, and explicitly states when it cannot find a grounded answer, this now specifically includes questions about the Section 5 items downgraded this pass (Art. 80 self-convening, the 100%-attendance claim), where the honest answer is "unconfirmed," not a guess.
3. Separately, the rules engine proactively raises an alert when a structural fact changes past a threshold.
4. **Friction point:** alert fatigue, the system must distinguish "must act now" from "will become a violation in 90 days," with clearly different visual treatment.
5. **Ends:** Youssef either resolves the flagged item or formally acknowledges/schedules it, with that action logged.
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-19, FR-20, FR-21, FR-22.

### Journey F, Convene and vote at a General Assembly (Sara, Shareholder Relations Officer)
1. **Trigger:** the annual GA is due, or an extraordinary matter (e.g., a capital increase) requires an EGM.
2. Sara opens the Shareholder Registry, confirms current shareholding percentages, and selects OGM or EGM.
3. The system validates the notice period against Section 5's rules and computes the first- and second-meeting quorum thresholds; Sara schedules the required newspaper-publication notices as tracked external tasks with deadlines, since actual publication happens outside the platform.
4. The agenda is built with each item tagged by topic category, which pre-selects the applicable voting threshold, the same mechanism as Journey B's step 4, reused here for GA topics.
5. The GA convenes, in-person or hybrid via the embedded conferencing module; shareholders or their proxies check in, and live quorum is computed by shareholding percentage present, not headcount.
6. Votes are cast weighted by shareholding and tallied immutably, using the same Vote/Resolution model as board meetings with a non-unity weight.
7. **Friction point, explicit MVP boundary:** a shareholding held through an omnibus or custodian account cannot be individually verified without a registrar/MCDR integration, out of MVP scope. The system flags these as "registrar-verification pending" rather than silently accepting an unverifiable holding.
8. **Ends:** GA resolutions are recorded; disclosure obligations flow into Journey D exactly as they do for board resolutions.
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-29, FR-30, FR-41, FR-6.

### Journey G, From board-composition gap to appointed director (Nourhan + Yara, candidate)
1. **Trigger:** the automatic composition check (FR-4) flags that the company needs one more independent NED to meet FRA Decree 100's ratio, following an existing director's resignation.
2. Nourhan converts the alert directly into an open board-seat posting (FR-33); the system pre-fills the required criteria from the exact rule that triggered the alert.
3. Yara, whose public profile shows her sector expertise and current independence status (pulled from her existing verified bord capacity elsewhere), sees the posting and expresses interest (FR-34).
4. Nourhan reviews Yara's profile and disclosed board positions; the system cross-checks those positions against the same independence rule that generated the original alert, before Nourhan has to ask, automatically and reliably for any other company that's also a bord tenant, since that cross-check runs against real `Capacity` records, not a claim.
5. **Friction point:** a candidate with no existing bord footprint can still apply with a self-declared profile (built during her own individual onboarding, Journey I), but her independence status shows as "self-declared, unverified" rather than the verified badge Yara's bord history earns her. Reconfirmed this pass (Section 5): no GAFI or FRA registry exists that would let this be automated for positions outside bord's own tenant base, so this distinction is a permanent, honest platform boundary, not a temporary gap.
6. Nourhan appoints Yara; this creates a new Capacity record directly from the accepted application, no re-entry (FR-35), attaching to Yara's existing individual bord identity rather than creating a duplicate.
7. **Ends:** Yara is a live capacity holder, the original compliance alert is resolved, and the appointment is logged.
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-4, FR-32, FR-33, FR-34, FR-35.

### Journey H, Remuneration approval and payout-export cycle
1. **Trigger:** the quarterly remuneration review cycle, or a new director's appointment triggers an initial remuneration-structure assignment.
2. The Remuneration & Compensation committee (or the combined Nomination & Remuneration committee, per the applicable regulator's mandated structure) reviews proposed retainer/per-meeting/committee-premium rates, referencing each director's actual attendance record already captured by the platform's live quorum tracking (Journey C).
3. The proposal goes to the board (or GA, where legally required) for a vote; the existing recusal logic automatically excludes each director from voting on their own compensation item, per Art. 74.
4. Once approved, the calculated amount posts as `CreditTransaction` earn events by default (Section 15's credits model), spendable immediately against the corporate-partner catalog; once bord's cash-redemption flag is set, the same recipient can instead request a cash redemption, which the Secretary or Youssef then batches into a payout-ready export (bank-file or accounting-system format), using the bank details each recipient supplied during their own individual onboarding (Journey I, step 5). **For MVP, bord calculates and exports; it does not move money itself**, but the export format and underlying data model are deliberately structured so a Phase 2 payment-execution layer can consume the same records without re-modeling them (Section 11, Section 15).
5. **Friction point:** a director holding two capacities at the same company (e.g., NED and committee chair) needs the calculation to correctly sum both fee components without double-counting a shared retainer, a concrete case the calculation logic must handle explicitly, not an edge case left implicit.
6. **Ends:** the export is handed to the company's own finance/payroll function for execution today, with the same record ready to feed a native payout-execution flow once that Phase 2 capability exists; the approved remuneration record is archived in the audit log for the next disclosure cycle.
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-36, FR-37, FR-38, FR-39, FR-12, FR-53 (credits-posting default, step 4, new this pass), FR-54/FR-55 (corporate-partner and funding-gated cash redemption, step 4, new this pass).

### Journey I, Individual onboarding (any new person joining the platform)
1. **Trigger:** a person arrives at bord either by accepting a company's invitation (from Journey A/G) or independently, wanting a portable governance identity/network profile before any company has invited them.
2. They create a personal account, decoupled from any one company from the first step, consistent with the platform's person/capacity split (Section 8).
3. They provide personal information (name, contact details, national ID) and professional information (bio, expertise, current and past positions) as structured profile fields, not a free-text resume upload.
4. They complete **KYC identity verification** as a distinct, separately-labeled step: KYC confirms who they are, and explicitly nothing more, it is not confirmation of any specific board or committee position. The UI keeps this visually distinct from capacity verification at every point it's shown, never collapsed into one generic "verified" badge.
5. At this same step, they **declare every board, committee, and GA position they currently hold across every company**, not only companies already on bord. This self-declaration is the actual mechanism that makes affiliation and conflict-of-interest checking possible at all outside bord's own tenant base, given the confirmed absence of any external searchable registry (Section 5). It feeds both their optional public network profile (FR-32) and the conflict/independence cross-check run whenever a bord-tenant company considers appointing them (Journey G, step 4). Each declaration is architected to support a future `ExternalVerificationCheck` (FR-56) against a regulator or labor/social-insurance database, should one ever become accessible, but no such check runs today, and the UI never implies one has.
6. They provide **bank details for remuneration purposes**, encrypted and stored under the same data-residency and access-control discipline as national-ID data, needed so the payout-ready export Journey H produces is actually usable, even though MVP only exports rather than executes payment.
7. **Friction point:** a senior, time-poor persona (Karim, Persona 2) may abandon a lengthy onboarding form; the flow must let personal/professional info, KYC, position declaration, and bank details be completed as separable steps at different times, not one blocking form, with a clear "profile completeness" indicator rather than an all-or-nothing gate.
8. **Ends:** the person has a durable bord identity with three visually distinct trust layers, KYC-verified identity, real verified-`Capacity` positions wherever bord already knows them, and self-declared claims everywhere else, ready to be invited into a company (Journey A) or to build a standalone network profile (Journey G).
**Requirement cross-check:** drives FR-49 (individual account/profile creation), FR-50 (KYC verification, distinct from capacity verification), FR-51 (self-declared cross-company position capture), FR-52 (encrypted bank-detail capture for remuneration), FR-32 (reused, public profile built from this data).

## 10. Goals & Success Metrics

Given the platform runs on the current date (September 2026) and the lean-team, MVP-fast constraint, and given the user's own framing of success (Section 0 interview: all four dimensions below matter together, not just one):

- **Paying, live usage, 6 months post-launch:** 8–15 paying/design-partner companies live (Section 6, Year 1 SOM), at least 3 of which are FRA-regulated NBFIs, each running at least one real board meeting cycle end-to-end (Journeys B–D) on the platform.
- **Paying, live usage, 12 months post-launch:** 25–40 companies live, with visible renewal/retention signal from the Year 1 cohort; zero cross-tenant data-leakage incidents; at least one governance-advisory firm (Persona 6 type) onboarded as a multi-client reseller/admin; at least one board-seat posting filled end-to-end through the hiring module as the first proof the network layer produces a real outcome, not just registered profiles.
- **Regulatory/legal defensibility:** by 6 months, local counsel and at least one pilot company's compliance officer (Persona 5 type) have reviewed and signed off that the rules engine's High-confidence rules and the e-voting/minutes audit trail hold up under real regulatory scrutiny, a substantive review, not a rubber stamp, and one that explicitly covers how the Section 5 Medium/Low-confidence items (Art. 80's self-convening detail, the CBE rule set) are presented so they're never mistaken for settled law. By 12 months, the audit trail should have been tested against at least one real board dispute, regulator inquiry, or dry-run equivalent, and survived it.
- **Fundraising-ready traction:** by 6–12 months, the combination of live paying usage, retention signal, and legal sign-off above should be sufficient to support a seed raise, if pursued, this metric is a byproduct of the other three holding true, not a separate initiative.
- **Revenue:** tracked against the Section 6 SOM-implied range (Year 1 pilot pricing through the low end of the Year 2 band); no fixed EGP/MRR target was specified as a hard number, so revenue is read as a trend against that bottom-up range rather than a single go/no-go figure.
- **Leading indicators tracked from day one:** average time-to-schedule a compliant meeting, number of compliance violations caught before becoming a live regulatory issue, AI governance-assistant answer grounding rate, the acknowledgment rate on pre-vote conflict flags (a rate near zero suggests the check isn't finding anything real; a rate that gets reflexively dismissed every time suggests alert fatigue is setting in), and, new this pass, individual-onboarding completion rate broken out by step (personal/professional info vs. KYC vs. position declaration vs. bank details), since Journey I's own friction point (step 7) predicts step-level drop-off is likely and worth watching specifically rather than only tracking overall signup completion.

## 11. Scope

### In Scope (MVP)
- Multi-tenant company/entity onboarding with regulatory profile selection (JSC / FRA-NBFI subtype / CBE-regulated bank) plus an orthogonal EGX-listed flag that can combine with any profile
- Document vault with AI-assisted structural extraction (human-confirmed), **including upload and extraction of historical board/GA minutes and resolutions specifically to seed the precedent database at onboarding**
- **A decoupled individual-onboarding flow**: personal/professional profile, KYC identity verification (kept visually distinct from capacity verification), self-declared cross-company position capture, and encrypted bank-detail capture for remuneration
- Governance structure builder with multi-capacity person modeling, attaching to existing individual bord identities where they exist rather than duplicating them
- Availability-aware meeting scheduling with cross-capacity conflict detection, calendar sync (Google Calendar, Microsoft 365) combined with standing timing preferences to propose concrete slot options
- Rules-engine-based quorum, notice-period, and voting-threshold validation, keyed by governance body and topic category, configurable per company's rules profile, **with the Art. 80 self-convening mechanism and the 100%-attendance notice claim explicitly modeled as unconfirmed/configurable, not asserted defaults, per Section 5**
- Smart agenda builder with recurring compliance-calendar suggestions, a taxonomy of common agenda topics with editable preset template language, personalized to a company's own frequent items over time, plus an auto-generated discussion guide per substantive agenda item
- A configurable member-requisitioned-meeting threshold, defaulting to Companies Law Art. 80's one-third-of-directors trigger (the requisition right itself, not the unconfirmed self-convening detail) at board level, per company's AoA/bylaws
- A full committee taxonomy: required committees (audit, risk, nomination & remuneration; governance duties may sit inside the audit committee per Section 5's reconfirmed FRA reading) gated by regulatory profile, plus discretionary committee templates that stand up only once approved by board resolution
- A voluntary, self-certified compliance score and exportable certificate for companies outside mandatory FRA/CBE/EGX jurisdiction
- A pre-agenda-item and pre-vote check against law, AoA, bylaws, and prior resolutions, advisory-only, never blocking
- A smart, searchable database of past meetings and resolutions, seeded at onboarding rather than starting empty
- Embedded video conferencing with a manual roll-call fallback mode
- Live quorum tracking and in-meeting e-voting, including recusal/conflict-of-interest logic
- AI-assisted draft minutes generation with mandatory human review/approval
- Immutable, hash-chained audit log
- Compliance Center: proactive violation alerts + grounded AI governance Q&A with source citation
- Automatic disclosure-deadline detection and filing-ready (not direct-submission) exports
- Closely-held/mid-size GA (shareholder) meeting management: shareholder registry, GA scheduling, shareholding-weighted quorum and voting, without proxy solicitation or broker/custodian reconciliation
- A minimal professional-network layer: public professional profiles built from verified capacity history where available and self-declared data from individual onboarding otherwise, board/committee-seat job postings tied to a company's own composition-gap alerts, candidate expression-of-interest, and direct conversion of an accepted candidate into a Capacity record
- A remuneration management module: remuneration-structure definition, attendance-based fee calculation, the existing conflict-of-interest-aware approval workflow, and a payout-ready export, **with the data model and export format deliberately designed to support a Phase 2 payment-execution layer (Section 15), even though MVP itself does not execute payouts**
- Bilingual (Arabic/English) UI with Arabic as the authoritative legal-document language
- Role-based access control scoped per capacity, with strict tenant data isolation
- Per-company subscription billing (tiered by regulatory profile/complexity)
- Web app + native mobile apps (iOS/Android)

### Out of Scope (MVP)
- **Direct regulatory system integration/submission**, MVP produces filing-ready exports only
- **Full public-market AGM infrastructure**, proxy solicitation, broker/custodian vote reconciliation, and MCDR (central depository/registrar) integration are explicitly deferred; the data model is architected so this can be added without a rewrite, but none of it is built in MVP
- **The full professional network**, messaging, a content feed, and a general connections graph beyond the hiring flow are deferred; MVP ships only public profiles and board-seat postings
- **Remuneration payout execution**, MVP calculates and exports; **this is now an explicit Phase 2 feature, not a permanent exclusion (Section 15)**, the data model and export format are built this revision specifically so a later payment-execution layer doesn't require a rewrite
- **Real integration with credit-rating agencies or EGX's listing process**, the voluntary compliance score (FR-48) is a self-certified export a company can present to a third party itself; bord builds no outbound data-sharing pipeline or formal partnership toward either
- **Automated cross-registry affiliation search**, reconfirmed this pass (Section 5): no GAFI/FRA/EGX registry exists that supports person-level board-affiliation search; conflict/verification checking stays scoped to bord's own tenant data plus the individual-onboarding self-declaration (Journey I) as the honest fallback
- **Automated legal advice beyond grounded Q&A**, the AI assistant never generates novel legal interpretations or recommends specific board actions as legal advice; this covers the discussion-guide generator, the pre-vote conflict check, and the onboarding document-extraction step, all of which are advisory/human-confirmed, never authoritative
- **Multi-country expansion**, Egypt-only rules engine and regulatory scope for MVP
- **Offline-first full functionality**, read-only cached access to upcoming agenda/documents is supported offline; live voting and conferencing require connectivity
- **Wet-signature/notarization workflow automation**, until E-Signature Law 15/2004's full scope is confirmed with counsel
- **External verification of self-declared positions against a regulator or labor database** *(new this pass)*, no GAFI/FRA/EGX board-affiliation API or NOSI/labor-office employment-verification channel exists today (Section 5); FR-56's `ExternalVerificationCheck` is architected now so this can be added without a schema change, but nothing in this build ever calls out to such a source or claims a self-declared position was externally checked
- **Cash redemption of earned credits before bord secures funding** *(new this pass)*, credits are earned and partner-redeemable from MVP; cash redemption is gated behind a platform-wide flag bord's own ops team sets once, after a qualifying funding event (Section 15), never a per-company or per-user toggle

## 13. Edge Cases & Failure Scenarios

Organized by category; only categories materially relevant to this product are included. Each item is concrete and testable. Items marked *(new)* are additions this pass, mostly stemming from the two onboarding journeys.

**Identity & auth**
- A person holds multiple capacities across multiple companies under one login; switching active "capacity context" mid-session must not leak the previous company's data into the new context's view or cache.
- A director is removed/deactivated from a company mid-term; their historical votes, minutes, and audit-log entries must remain intact and attributed.
- Shared-device boardroom login must not be treated as a single identity for vote attribution.
- The Head of Internal Audit's dual reporting line (functionally to the Audit Committee, administratively to the CEO, Persona 9) must be modeled explicitly in the structure builder, not collapsed into a single reporting-line field.
- *(new)* A person completes individual onboarding (Journey I) and is later invited to a company (Journey A/G) using a different contact detail (e.g., a personal email vs. a corporate one), the invitation-acceptance flow must be able to match/merge to their existing bord identity rather than silently creating a duplicate person record.
- *(new)* KYC verification fails or times out mid-onboarding, the person's account must persist in a clearly-labeled "KYC pending/failed" state rather than blocking all platform access outright, since a legitimate person can be invited into a company (read-only, non-voting capacity) before KYC completes, but must not be able to cast a vote or e-sign until it does.

**Data integrity & concurrency**
- Two people edit the same agenda simultaneously before a meeting, requires optimistic locking with a merge/conflict surface, not last-write-wins.
- A vote is cast during a network drop and the client is unsure whether it registered, the system must be idempotent on vote submission.
- Quorum is recalculated live as attendees join/leave mid-meeting; a resolution passed at a moment when quorum was technically not met must be flagged, not silently accepted.
- *(new)* AI-assisted extraction during company onboarding (FR-2/FR-2a) proposes a structural fact from an uploaded document that conflicts with a fact the secretary manually enters in the structure builder in the same session, the system must surface the conflict for explicit resolution, not silently prefer one source over the other.

**Offline & connectivity**
- A director locally approves an agenda item while offline, the timestamp used for deadline calculations must be the server-received time, not device-local.
- Boardroom in a low-connectivity area needs at least read access to the agenda and documents offline; live voting/conferencing degrade to the manual roll-call fallback.

**Scale & performance**
- A GA-type meeting with capital-weighted voting across many shareholders requires a different tally engine path than a small per-capita board vote, both must be supported without one degrading the other.
- A single multi-client admin (Persona 6) with 15–20 client companies must get portfolio-level views without N+1-query performance collapse.
- A large-shareholder-count GA (beyond the closely-held MVP boundary) currently has no supported path, the system must clearly reject or flag this case rather than silently mishandling it.
- *(new)* A company with a very large historical-minutes backlog (years of documents) uploaded at onboarding (FR-2a) must have extraction processed asynchronously with visible progress, not block the rest of onboarding on full extraction completing.

**Localization**
- Arabic is the authoritative legal-document language; English translations are working copies only.
- RTL/LTR mixed layouts must render correctly in both the web and mobile apps.

**Accessibility**
- Senior board members need large-text, low-navigation-depth modes, including in the remuneration-approval, hiring, and onboarding screens, not only the original meeting flows.

**Abuse, fraud & security**
- Vote/e-signature impersonation risk: MFA required specifically at the moment of voting/signing.
- Confidential board-pack leakage: access logging plus identity watermarking as a deterrent, not a technical prevention guarantee.
- Prompt injection into the AI governance assistant, the discussion-guide generator, the pre-vote conflict check, or the onboarding document-extraction step via uploaded documents must not cause any of them to deviate from grounded, citation-only/human-confirmed behavior.
- A fabricated or exaggerated candidate profile on the network layer, the "verified vs. self-declared" distinction is the primary control, but the platform must also make it visually unmistakable which label applies.
- Reputational/defamation exposure from the network layer, even in its minimal MVP form, needs a defined moderation/dispute contact point before launch.
- A voluntary compliance certificate (FR-48) gets presented by a company as if it were an official regulatory endorsement or a real credit-rating outcome, the certificate's own wording must state plainly that it's a self-assessment against bord's rule set.
- A discretionary committee's enabling board resolution passes without the quorum the resolution vote itself required, the same quorum-at-moment-of-vote edge case as above, worth naming explicitly since a wrongly-instantiated committee then starts generating its own agenda items on a compromised foundation.
- *(new)* A person self-declares a board position at a company they do not actually hold a seat at (Journey I, step 5), since no external registry exists to verify this (Section 5), the platform's only mitigating control is that a bord-tenant company's own cross-check (Journey G, step 4) only ever treats *bord-tenant* capacities as verified; a false self-declared claim about a non-tenant company can mislead a company relying on the public profile, and the UI's "self-declared, unverified" labeling (FR-32) is the sole defense, this must never be watered down to look more authoritative than it is.
- *(new)* Bank details captured for remuneration (FR-52) are a high-value target if the account is compromised, access to view/export bank details must require step-up authentication beyond ordinary session login, separate from and in addition to the platform's general MFA-at-voting requirement.

**Third-party dependency failure**
- Video SDK outage during a live vote: manual roll-call fallback must be reachable within the same meeting-room UI.
- A connected Google Calendar/Microsoft 365 account has its sync token expire or gets revoked outside bord, the scheduler must degrade to standing-timing-preferences-only slot suggestion, flag the stale connection to the user, and never silently propose slots against calendar data that's gone out of sync.
- AI governance-guidance API outage must not block rules-engine-driven proactive alerts, this also applies to the discussion-guide generator, the pre-vote check, and the onboarding extraction step: an LLM outage should degrade those features gracefully (skip, with a clear "not checked/not extracted" state) rather than block agenda publishing, meeting scheduling, or onboarding entirely.
- *(new)* The KYC-verification vendor is unavailable, onboarding must not be entirely blocked; the person's account persists in "KYC pending" state (see Identity & auth above) and retries automatically, rather than the whole onboarding flow failing.

**Legal/compliance edge cases**
- A director recused from a vote, whether they still count toward quorum presence is legally ambiguous; exposed as a configurable, unset-by-default setting per company.
- A departed director requests data deletion under PDPL, governance records are retained per the stated interim policy; personal data within them is minimized/de-identified where legally possible, the record itself is not deleted.
- A company's regulatory profile changes mid-subscription, the rules profile and applicable committee mandates must be re-evaluated and the company re-flagged for a compliance gap review.
- A bank customer relies on a Medium/Low-confidence CBE rule that later proves incorrect once primary-source text is obtained, the platform must be able to identify every company whose compliance status was evaluated against a since-corrected rule value and re-flag them, not just apply the fix going forward silently.
- *(new)* A company relies on the Section 5-downgraded Art. 80 self-convening default (configured "on" before counsel confirms it) and a self-convened meeting's validity is later challenged, the platform must retain the exact `RuleVersion` and configuration state in effect at the time, the same snapshot pattern used for every other rule-dependent decision, so the company (and bord) can show precisely what default was active and that it was disclosed as unconfirmed, not asserted as settled law.
- *(new)* An individual withdraws or amends a self-declared position (FR-51) after a company has already relied on it in an appointment decision (Journey G), the change must be timestamped and versioned, and any company that relied on the prior declaration in an active appointment process should see the change surfaced, not have it silently disappear from history.

**Multi-tenancy**
- A person serves as NED at Company A and committee member at Company B, no data from Company A must be visible in Company B's context.
- A corporate group with a parent and subsidiaries sharing some directors: the group-tenant model must let a group-level admin see structural relationships without collapsing confidential per-subsidiary data.
- A corporate secretarial firm's admin account (Persona 6) manages many client tenants, compromise of that account must not cascade into all client tenants.
- A director's public network profile is visible across tenants by design (it has to be, to function as a marketplace), while every other governance record stays strictly tenant-isolated, this deliberate, narrow exception must be enforced at the data-model level, not just by UI convention.
- *(new)* An individual's onboarding data (Journey I: KYC status, self-declared positions, bank details) lives outside any single company tenant by design, since the whole point is decoupling identity from company, this is the *second* deliberate exception to strict tenant isolation (the network profile being the first), and it must be scoped just as narrowly: a company can see a candidate's *public* profile and the specific declared positions relevant to an independence check it's actively running, never the person's KYC internals or bank details, which belong to the person alone.

## 14. Non-Functional Requirements

- **Performance:** meeting-room live quorum/vote updates must reflect within 2 seconds of a state change under normal connectivity.
- **Scale (MVP planning basis):** designed for the Section 6 SOM range (up to ~120 companies by Year 3, each with roughly 10–15 people holding capacities), not designed for large-scale public-shareholder AGM voting at MVP.
- **Security/privacy:** MFA mandatory for voting/signing actions; step-up authentication for bank-detail access (new this pass); encryption at rest and in transit; hash-chained immutable audit log; Egypt/MENA-region data residency by default; PDPC-registered DPO in place before processing personal data at scale.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.1 AA at minimum for the web app, with particular attention to large-text/low-navigation-depth modes for senior board members.
- **Availability:** the compliance rules engine and alerting must remain available independent of the AI/LLM layer and the video-conferencing SDK, this explicitly includes the discussion-guide generator, pre-vote conflict check, and onboarding document-extraction step, all AI-layer features that must degrade gracefully.
- **Data integrity:** votes and minutes are append-only/immutable once finalized; corrections require a documented amendment record.
- **Governance-record retention:** indefinite, or minimum 10 years post-subscription-cancellation, per Section 5's interim policy, pending counsel confirmation of a definitive statutory period.

## 15. Remuneration: Phase 1 (calculate & export) and Phase 2 (execute)

Called out as its own short section because it's the single item corrected by explicit user feedback on the prior draft, and the correction changes a design constraint, not just wording.

**Phase 1 (this build, MVP):** bord defines remuneration structures per capacity, calculates fees from real attendance data, routes approval through the standard vote/recusal workflow, and produces a payout-ready export (FR-36–FR-39). It does not move money. This is the same functional scope as the prior draft.

**What's different this pass:** the prior draft described payout execution as a scope boundary bord was "never" going to cross, a permanent exclusion. That framing was corrected: **payout execution is a genuine Phase 2 feature**, planned to build on the same data captured in Phase 1 (the remuneration records, the bank details collected during individual onboarding, FR-52, and the export schema itself), not a reason to under-model that data now. Concretely, this means:

- The remuneration `Record`/`Export` data model (ARCHITECTURE.md) is versioned and structured so a payment-execution service could consume it directly as an instruction set, rather than requiring a reverse-engineered translation layer built later.
- Bank details are collected and encrypted to a standard suitable for eventual payment-initiation use (Section 14), not just for populating a static export file.
- The export format itself is chosen for compatibility with common Egyptian bank-file/payroll formats specifically because that same format is the natural input to a future execution layer, not only because it's convenient for a human to hand off today.
- Phase 2 execution is explicitly deferred out of this MVP's functional requirements and roadmap (Section 11, Section 18), it is not being built now, but it is a stated, intended next phase, not a hypothetical. The roadmap's Phase 9 (Post-Launch & Iteration) is where a build decision on Phase 2 execution belongs, informed by real usage of the Phase 1 export.

### Credits-based earning model, a standalone incentive layer (new this pass)

The request going into this pass was to "apply the AfterQuery model": pay in credits redeemable through corporate partners rather than cash, until bord raises funding, with optional cash redemption afterward. Researched directly this pass: AfterQuery (afterquery.com, YC-backed) does not use a credits model, its own documentation states contributors are paid entirely in cash via Stripe, weekly, direct to bank account, with no points economy and no funding-gated cash conversion. What's built here is the credits/points-economy structure as described, independent of AfterQuery's actual (cash-based) model, confirmed with the requester before building. Confirmed with the requester a second time on scope: rather than scoping credits narrowly to remuneration, they're built as a standalone incentive layer spanning multiple modules, a `RemunerationStructure` schedule is one source of earn events among several, not the mechanism itself.

**Mechanic (FR-53-55):** while `PlatformSettings.cashRedemptionEnabled` is false, several governance-positive activities post `CreditTransaction` earn events instead of cash or in addition to no compensation at all, at the platform's current credit-to-EGP notional rate: a `RemunerationStructure`'s calculated retainer, per-meeting fee, and committee premium (the original remuneration-replacement case); meeting participation and timely resolution execution; compliance-task completion; and referral bonuses, none of which are tied to any one company or `RemunerationStructure`. Credits are spendable immediately against the `CorporatePartner` catalog (training/certification providers, software tools, travel, retail; bord-curated, platform-wide, not per-company). `cashRedemptionEnabled` is a single platform-wide flag, set once by bord's own ops team after a qualifying funding event, never a per-company or per-user setting, this is a deliberate one-way door (ARCHITECTURE-ESSENTIALS.md §1). Once set, `CreditRedemption` rows of type `cash` become creatable, and are reconciled into the existing Phase 2 `PayoutExport` design above via `PayoutExportLineItem.creditRedemptionId`, the one documented connection point between the standalone incentives layer and the remuneration module's cash-execution pipeline, so bord doesn't need two separate payout pipelines.

**What's deliberately not solved this pass:** the credit-to-EGP rate-setting process (who sets it, how often, what governs a change) is bord's own pricing decision, not a compliance value, and is left as an open item (Section 17) rather than encoded as policy here. Corporate-partner sourcing and offer curation is a business-development function outside this document's scope. The exact trigger rules for `meeting_participation`, `timely_resolution_execution`, and `compliance_task_completion` earn events (how many credits, for which specific action) are intentionally left as an implementation-phase product decision, not specified numerically here.

## 15a. Governance Integrity: Independence Classification, Conflict Detection, and Committee Decision Quorums (new this pass)

Three related capabilities, all advisory rather than enforcement mechanisms, grouped here because they share one architectural theme: bord can now flag a governance-integrity concern with a specific, sourced reason, but it never blocks a vote, a resolution, or a capacity assignment on the strength of that flag. This mirrors the existing pre-vote conflict check (FR-44) and the Rules Engine's general confidence-flagging discipline (Section 5): where Egyptian regulation is genuinely unsettled or silent, the product surfaces that honestly rather than pretending to enforce a rule that isn't actually written down.

**Independence classification and the miscategorization check (FR-57).** Every `Capacity` can now be classified `independent`, `shareholder_representative`, `executive`, or `non_executive_other` (`directorCategory`, ARCHITECTURE.md §4). Egyptian regulation affirmatively defines "independent" (Section 5); it does not affirmatively define "shareholder representative", so the rules engine can meaningfully check an `independent`-classified capacity against the sourced criteria (shareholding threshold, material relationship, family relationship, cooling-off period, tenure), producing `independenceComplianceStatus: 'flagged'` and a `ComplianceAlert` when it fails. The reverse case, a capacity classified `shareholder_representative` that in fact meets every independence criterion, is also flagged, since a company might misuse the label to route a de facto independent seat around a quota it doesn't want counted. Both directions are `warning` severity only, never `violation`, and never block the assignment (the explicit decision behind this build, see ARCHITECTURE-ESSENTIALS.md §1). `representedShareholderId` links a `shareholder_representative` capacity to the specific `Shareholder` it represents, required for the pattern detection below to mean anything.

**Voting-pattern conflict detection (FR-58-59).** Operating over `Vote`/`Resolution` history, the rules engine produces two kinds of `GovernanceConflictFlag`: pattern-level (`consistent_split_votes`, the same bloc of capacities voting together against the same opposing bloc across a rolling threshold of resolutions; `independent_vs_representative_opposition`, `independent`-classified capacities voting opposite `shareholder_representative`-classified capacities across that same threshold, the specific "ownership malicious interference and abuse of power" signal named in the request) and incident-level (`mislabel_incident`, fired individually, never batched, every time a capacity with a `'flagged'` independence status casts a vote on the same side as one or more representative capacities against one or more independent capacities on a single resolution, watching specifically for an independent-labeled director who is, in substance, "a representative in disguise"). Visibility is full-board by explicit decision, confirmed with the requester over the initially-proposed restricted default: any capacity at the company can see a company's flags, ordinary tenant isolation applies and no additional role gate does. What is role-restricted is proactive notification, not read access: a `system_admin` capacity (a new RoleType this pass, ARCHITECTURE.md §4, the company's own bord-tenant administrator) is pushed a notification on every new flag and every `independent_director_criteria` alert, in addition to full-board visibility, not instead of it. `compliance_officer` (also new this pass) is the role expected to actually review and set status on a flag, a workflow-ownership designation, not a visibility restriction.

**Committee-level compositional decision quorum (FR-60).** A company can set, per committee, a minimum count or percentage of independent-member votes required for that committee's decisions to pass (`Committee.decisionQuorum`, ARCHITECTURE.md §4), on top of and independent from ordinary attendance quorum. This has no Egyptian statutory citation, the regulatory research this pass found board-level independence ratios but no committee-level minimum-independent-votes-to-pass mechanism, so it is modeled and labeled explicitly as company policy (`sourceCitation: 'company_policy'`), not encoded as a `RuleVersion` legal value. A committee resolution's `Resolution.compositionQuorumMet` is a second, independent gate alongside `quorumMet`, both must clear for the resolution to pass.

- **Mislabel-incident false-positive rate, and the stakes are higher than a restricted-visibility design would have carried** *(new)*: FR-58's incident-level flag fires on every vote matching the pattern, with no accumulation threshold, by design (the request was to flag every incident, not wait for a pattern). A genuinely independent director who happens to agree with a representative director on a specific resolution's merits will trigger the same flag as deliberate collusion. FR-59's full-board visibility (confirmed explicitly with the requester over an initially-proposed restricted default) means a false positive is immediately visible to the entire board, not filtered through a compliance officer first, this is a real, accepted tradeoff of the transparency decision, not an oversight, but it raises the real-world cost of a false positive materially. **Needs Phase 1/7 validation that a false `mislabel_incident` flag doesn't itself become a source of board conflict, on top of validating detection accuracy.**
- **Committee decisionQuorum interaction with existing attendance-quorum edge cases** *(new)*: Section 13's "quorum dropped right before the vote" edge case was designed against attendance quorum only. **Needs an explicit pass to confirm compositionQuorumMet inherits the same live-recompute behavior**, not a separate, potentially inconsistent check.
